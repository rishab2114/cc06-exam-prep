Week 1

Why Everyone Should Know About Sustainability

Introduction to the concept of sustainability, the SDGs, climate change basics, and Singapore's green ambitions.

🌱 SDG 13: Climate Action ♻️ Circular Economy 🌳 Natural Climate Solutions
Sustainability means meeting today's needs without compromising future generations. The 17 UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) form the global blueprint. Climate change — driven by greenhouse gas emissions from fossil fuels — is the defining challenge, and countries must balance economic growth with environmental stewardship through circular models and nature-based solutions.
1.5°C Paris Agreement target above pre-industrial
420 ppm CO₂ in atmosphere today
2015 Paris Agreement signed
1 million+ trees to be planted in SG
🌡️ Earth is warming at ~0.2°C per decade — the last decade was the warmest on record. At this rate, 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels could be breached within 20 years.
🌍 What is Sustainability?
Brundtland Definition (1987)

Meeting the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

Three Pillars

Environmental integrity, Economic viability, Social equity — the three must be balanced simultaneously.

Complex Problems

Sustainability challenges are interdisciplinary, interconnected, and involve trade-offs. Solutions are rarely simple.

🎯 The 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)
People (SDGs 1–5)
  • No Poverty, Zero Hunger
  • Good Health, Quality Education
  • Gender Equality
Planet (SDGs 6, 13–15)
  • Clean Water & Sanitation
  • Climate Action
  • Life Below Water, Life on Land
Prosperity (SDGs 7–12)
  • Affordable Clean Energy
  • Decent Work, Innovation
  • Reduced Inequalities
Peace & Partnerships (16–17)
  • Peace, Justice & Strong Institutions
  • Global Partnerships for the Goals
♻️ Circular Economy
What it IS
  • Design out waste and pollution
  • Keep products and materials in use longer
  • Regenerate natural systems
What it is NOT

Simply offsetting emissions through tree planting or cleaning up pollution after the fact. That is still linear thinking.

SG Circular Ambition
  • Zero Waste Masterplan
  • Extended Producer Responsibility
  • Semakau Landfill — SG's only landfill
🌳 Natural Climate Solutions
Tree Planting (Million Trees)

Trees cool naturally through shading and releasing water via transpiration. They also sequester CO₂.

Carbon Sinks

Forests, mangroves, and wetlands absorb more CO₂ than they release — natural buffers against climate change.

⭐ Must-Know for Exams

  • Paris Agreement signed 2015 — first universal legally binding climate deal
  • Main source of CO₂: combustion of fossil fuels
  • Circular economy ≠ offset/compensate — it prevents waste at source
  • Trees as natural climate solution: shading + transpiration
  • Sustainable development problems are complex — NOT simple to solve
Week 2

No Poverty, Social Inclusion & Equality

Examining global poverty trends, inequality drivers, gender disparities, and the digital divide's role in widening gaps.

🏠 SDG 1: No Poverty ⚖️ SDG 10: Reduced Inequalities ♀ SDG 5: Gender Equality
Over 700 million people live in extreme poverty (<$2.15/day). Poverty is concentrated in Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia. Technology and the digital revolution are paradoxically widening the income gap. Social inclusion requires eliminating discrimination, enabling mobility, and ensuring basic needs — the gender gap goes far beyond pay.
>700M people in extreme poverty
$2.15/day World Bank poverty line
40% of global income to top 10%
SDG 1 No Poverty by 2030
📉 COVID-19 pushed ~100 million more people into extreme poverty in 2020, reversing decades of progress. The 2030 SDG poverty target is now off-track without accelerated action.
🗺️ Where is Poverty?
Sub-Saharan Africa

Highest concentration of extreme poverty. Conflict, climate shocks, governance challenges, and debt traps compound the problem.

South Asia

Large absolute numbers in poverty despite rapid growth. Inequality within countries is high.

Multidimensional Poverty

Beyond income: also includes lack of education, healthcare, clean water, sanitation, and electricity.

⚖️ Inequality & Social Inclusion
Social Inclusion Definition

Eliminate discrimination, enable high social mobility, ensure every human can meet basic needs. Focuses on vulnerable groups.

Digital Divide

Technology and digital revolution are widening the income gap. Those without access fall further behind.

Gini Coefficient

Measure of income inequality. 0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality. Singapore's Gini ~0.37 after taxes/transfers.

♀ Gender Inequality
Gender Pay Gap

Women earn less than men for equivalent work globally — but this is only ONE aspect of gender inequality.

Other Gender Dimensions
  • Political representation
  • Access to education
  • Violence & safety
  • Unpaid care work burden
SDG 5: Gender Equality

End all forms of discrimination, eliminate gender-based violence, ensure full participation in all spheres of life.

⭐ Must-Know for Exams

  • Poorest regions: Sub-Saharan Africa + South Asia
  • Social inclusion: eliminates discrimination, enables mobility, meets basic needs — True
  • Digital revolution is widening the income gap (not narrowing)
  • Gender pay gap is NOT the only aspect of gender inequality
  • SDG 1, 2, 10 all fall under poverty/inequality goals
Week 3

Introduction to Food Security & the Global Food System

From field to fork — understanding hunger, food waste, sustainable agriculture, and Singapore's food resilience strategies.

🌾 SDG 2: Zero Hunger 🔬 Sustainable Agriculture 🗑️ Food Waste
Despite enough food being produced globally, nearly 800 million people go hungry. The food system — from extraction to disposal — contributes ~25% of global GHG emissions. A third of all food is lost or wasted. Singapore imports 90% of its food, making diversification and local production critical for food security.
~828M undernourished globally
⅓ food produced is lost/wasted (FAO 2020)
90% Singapore imports its food
27% habitable land for livestock
7% habitable land for crops (human)
🐄 Livestock uses 27% of habitable land but livestock products provide only ~18% of global calories — a massive land-use inefficiency. Shifting to plant-based diets could free up enormous land area.
🌍 Food Security: Four Pillars
Availability

Sufficient food physically present. Depends on production, imports, reserves.

Access

People can afford to buy or obtain food. Income, markets, prices matter.

Utilisation

Body can absorb nutrients from food. Depends on diet diversity, cooking, health.

Stability

Consistent access over time. Climate, conflict, economic shocks threaten stability.

🗑️ Food Loss & Waste
Scale of the Problem

FAO: ⅓ of all food produced is lost or wasted annually — equivalent to 1.3 billion tonnes/year.

Food Loss vs. Waste

Loss = occurs during production, storage, processing (developing countries). Waste = at retail/consumer level (developed countries).

GHG Impact

If food waste were a country, it would be the 3rd largest emitter of GHGs globally.

🇸🇬 Singapore Food Innovations
Black Soldier Fly (BSF)

BSF larvae convert food waste into nutritious compost. Also a protein-rich feed for livestock/fish. Reduces landfill waste.

30 by 30 Goal

SG aims to produce 30% of nutritional needs locally by 2030. Currently ~10%. Sky farms, vertical farming, aquaculture.

Diversification

Import from 180+ countries/regions to avoid over-reliance on any single source. Food resilience strategy.

📊 Land Use for Food
  • 50% of habitable land is used for agriculture total
  • 27% of habitable land is used for livestock (pasture + feed crops)
  • 7% of habitable land grows crops directly for human consumption
  • WFP 2020 Nobel Peace Prize — "For efforts to combat hunger, contribution to bettering conditions for conflict-affected areas"

⭐ Must-Know for Exams

  • Undernourished people: numbers increased from 2019–2020
  • WFP Nobel 2020: combat hunger + conflict-affected areas
  • FAO: ⅓ of produced food wasted/lost
  • Black Soldier Fly larvae: food waste → compost
  • Land: 27% livestock, 7% crops for humans
Week 4

Water — Availability & Sustainable Management

Global water stress, the challenge of freshwater scarcity, and Singapore's remarkable Four National Taps strategy.

💧 SDG 6: Clean Water 🇸🇬 NEWater 🌦️ Climate Uncertainty
Only 3% of Earth's water is fresh, and most of that is locked in glaciers. One quarter of the global population faces high water stress. Agriculture consumes ~70% of freshwater globally. Singapore — with no natural aquifers and limited land — has pioneered water security through diversification: local catchments, imported water (Malaysia), NEWater, and desalination.
3% of Earth's water is freshwater
¼ of world faces high water stress
70% freshwater used by agriculture
50% NEWater to meet SG demand by 2030
430M gallons/day SG water demand
🚰 Singapore produces ~430 million gallons of water per day from its four taps. NEWater — ultra-clean recycled wastewater — already supplies ~40% and is targeted to reach 50% by 2030. The Deep Tunnel Sewerage System (DTSS) is key infrastructure enabling NEWater.
🌍 Global Water Stress
Scarcity Drivers
  • Population growth
  • Climate change (hardest to predict)
  • Pollution of freshwater sources
  • Overextraction of groundwater
Water Stress Statistics

1 in 4 people globally face high baseline water stress. By 2050, could affect 40% of population.

Biggest User: Agriculture

~70% of freshwater withdrawals go to agriculture (irrigation). Industrial use ~20%, Municipal ~10%.

🇸🇬 Singapore's Four National Taps
  • 1
    Local Catchment Water — 17 reservoirs covering two-thirds of SG's land as catchment area. Rainwater collected island-wide.
  • 2
    Imported Water from Malaysia — via Johor River (NOT the Strait of Johor). Agreement valid until 2061. SG is reducing reliance.
  • 3
    NEWater — ultra-clean reclaimed water (treat → purify → test). Meets ~40% of needs today; target 50% by 2030. Used in industry + reservoirs + emergency drinking.
  • 4
    Desalinated Water — seawater processed through reverse osmosis. Energy-intensive but climate-resilient.
🏗️ Deep Tunnel Sewerage System (DTSS) — a massive underground system that efficiently conveys used water to reclamation plants, making NEWater production possible at scale. DTSS Phase 2 completed 2025.
🔑 Key Water Concepts
Water Demand Uncertainty

Climate change is the key factor that makes water demand hardest to predict — extreme droughts and floods disrupt supply.

Virtual Water

Hidden water embedded in goods. Beef: ~15,000L per kg. Importing food = importing virtual water.

Water-Energy-Food Nexus

Water, energy, and food systems are deeply interconnected. Saving water often saves energy too.

⭐ Must-Know for Exams

  • Water demand uncertainty: climate change
  • High water stress: one-quarter of world population
  • Biggest water consumer: agriculture
  • NEWater target: 50% of SG demand by 2030
  • NOT a national tap: Strait of Johor (actual source is Johor River)
Week 5

Energy & Climate Change (I) — Background, Technologies & Policy

Energy systems, greenhouse gases, the energy poverty challenge, and pathways to decarbonisation.

⚡ SDG 7: Clean Energy 🌡️ SDG 13: Climate Action 🔋 Mitigation & Adaptation
Energy is the backbone of the modern economy, but burning fossil fuels releases greenhouse gases (GHGs) that trap heat. CO₂ is the most important GHG by volume but not the only one (methane, N₂O, etc.). 800 million people lack electricity. The challenge: lift the poor while decarbonising — through clean energy, behaviour change, and fuel switching.
~800M people lack electricity (energy poverty)
73% GHG from energy sector
1.5–2°C Paris Agreement warming limits
CO₂, CH₄, N₂O main GHGs
🌡️ Greenhouse Gases & Climate Change
Greenhouse Effect

GHGs trap outgoing infrared radiation, warming Earth's surface. Natural process — but human emissions are supercharging it.

Key GHGs
  • CO₂ — fossil fuels, deforestation
  • CH₄ — livestock, landfills, gas leaks
  • N₂O — fertilisers, agriculture
  • F-gases — refrigerants, industry
CO₂ is NOT the Only GHG

Methane is 80× more potent than CO₂ over 20 years. N₂O is 265× more potent over 100 years. All must be addressed.

💡 Energy Poverty
Definition

Lack of access to sustainable modern energy services and products. Affects cooking, heating, education, health, economic opportunity.

Low-income Countries

People seeking access to energy for basic needs like cooking, lighting, and communications.

High-income Countries

People looking for further improvement in well-being through energy — not just access, but higher standards of living.

🔋 Mitigation vs. Adaptation
Mitigation

Reducing GHG emissions at source. Clean energy transition, efficiency, behaviour change, fuel switching.

Adaptation

Adjusting to climate impacts already happening or expected. Seawalls, drought-resistant crops, early warning systems.

Key Fact

Mitigation and adaptation are both needed simultaneously — mitigation does NOT make adaptation plans obsolete.

🌱 Emission Mitigation Strategies
  • Advanced technologies — CCS, nuclear, green hydrogen
  • Behaviour change — reduced consumption, diet shifts, travel choices
  • Clean energy — solar, wind, hydro replacing fossil fuels
  • Fuel shift — gas replacing coal, hydrogen replacing gas

⭐ Must-Know for Exams

  • High-income countries: looking for further improvement in well-being
  • CO₂ is NOT the only GHG (CH₄, N₂O also critical)
  • Energy poverty = lack of access to sustainable modern energy services
  • Adaptation plans are NOT scrapped after mitigation — both run in parallel
  • Mitigation toolkit: tech + behaviour + clean energy + fuel shift — all True
Week 6

Energy & Climate Change (II) — Policy Instruments & Economics

Carbon pricing, cap-and-trade systems, international agreements, and the economic case for climate action.

💰 Carbon Tax 📊 Cap & Trade 🌐 UNFCCC & Paris
Governments use a mix of regulatory and economic tools to reduce emissions. Market-based instruments like carbon taxes and cap-and-trade make polluting expensive. The UNFCCC (1992) established the global framework; the Paris Agreement (2015) set binding commitments. The Stern Review proved climate action is economically rational — the cost of inaction far exceeds the cost of action.
1992 UNFCCC signed
2015 Paris Agreement
China > USA CO₂ emissions in 2018
SG $25/tonne carbon tax (2024)
💰 Policy Instruments: Categories
Regulatory (Command & Control)

Emission standards, technology mandates, bans. Direct but can be inflexible. Example: banning coal plants.

Economic Incentive-Based

Market-based: cap-and-trade, carbon credits. Non-market: carbon tax, subsidies for clean tech.

Voluntary/Informational

Labels, voluntary agreements, public campaigns. Complements mandatory measures.

📊 Cap and Trade vs. Carbon Tax
FeatureCap & TradeCarbon Tax
How it worksSet total emission cap; trade permitsPrice per tonne of CO₂
CertaintyCertain quantity reductionCertain price, uncertain quantity
Polluter pays?YesYes
Least cost?Yes — emissions cut where cheapestDepends on tax level
SG example—SG Carbon Tax: $5/t (2019), $25/t (2024), rising to $80/t by 2030
🌐 International Climate Agreements
  • 1
    UNFCCC (1992) — First global framework for addressing climate change. Signed at Rio Earth Summit.
  • 2
    Kyoto Protocol (1997) — First binding emission targets for developed countries (Annex I). Not ratified by USA.
  • 3
    Paris Agreement (2015) — Universal, legally binding. NDCs from all countries. Limit warming to 1.5–2°C above pre-industrial.
📈 The Stern Review (2006)
Key Finding

Climate change is the greatest market failure in history. Cost of inaction (5–20% of global GDP) far exceeds cost of action (~1% of GDP).

Pro-Growth Strategy

The Stern Review argued that taking climate action IS a pro-growth strategy — investing in clean energy creates jobs and drives innovation.

🌏 Global CO₂ Emissions
🏭 In 2018, China was the world's largest CO₂ emitter, emitting more than the United States. China ~28%, USA ~15% of global CO₂ emissions. However, on a per-capita basis, USA still emits significantly more per person than China.

⭐ Must-Know for Exams

  • Economic instruments: cap-and-trade + carbon tax (market & non-market)
  • Cap & Trade: polluter pays + lowest cost mitigation — both True
  • CO₂ emissions 2018: China > USA
  • Stern Review: climate action is a pro-growth strategy
  • UNFCCC signed: 1992
Week 7

Pollution — Air, Light, Noise & Plastic

Types of pollution, health impacts, Singapore's environmental challenges, and the global plastic crisis.

🌬️ Air Quality 💡 Light Pollution 🔊 Noise Pollution 🧴 Plastic Waste
Pollution takes many forms — air, light, noise, and plastic — each with distinct health and ecological impacts. Air pollution causes 7 million deaths/year (WHO). Singapore recycles only 4% of plastic. Light pollution grows 2%/year globally, disrupting ecosystems and human health. Noise affects not just hearing but cardiovascular health and wildlife.
7M deaths/year from air pollution (WHO)
4% plastic recycled in SG (2021)
67 dB max noise 1hr at home (SG)
2%/year light pollution growth globally
🌫️ The Great Smog of London (1952) killed ~12,000 people over five days — a watershed event that led to the UK's Clean Air Act (1956). A stark reminder of what unchecked air pollution can do.
🌬️ Air Pollution
Health Effects (Most to Least Serious)
  • Premature death, cancer
  • Heart & lung disease
  • Reduced lung function
  • Chest tightness (LEAST serious)
Main Pollutants
  • PM2.5 — fine particles, deepest lung penetration
  • NO₂ — vehicle exhaust
  • SO₂ — coal combustion
  • O₃ (ozone) — photochemical smog
Sources
  • Transport
  • Industry & power plants
  • Agricultural burning
  • Haze from regional fires (SG)
💡 Light Pollution
Diurnal Rhythm

Our body's 24-hour biological cycle — regulates sleep, hormones, metabolism. Light pollution disrupts melatonin production.

Ecological Impacts
  • Sea turtle hatchlings confused by lights
  • Bird migration disruption
  • Insect population decline
  • Plant flowering cycle affected
🔊 Noise Pollution
Marine Mammals

Dolphins and whales are extra sensitive to noise because they rely on sound (echolocation) for communication, navigation, and finding prey.

SG Noise Standards

Max permissible: 67 dB for 1 hour at home. Prolonged exposure to 85+ dB causes hearing damage. Road traffic is main source.

🧴 Plastic Pollution
SG Plastic Recycling

Only 4% of plastic recycled in Singapore as of 2021. Most goes to incineration or Semakau Landfill.

Microplastics

Plastics break down into microplastics (<5mm) that enter food chains, water supply, and have been found in human blood.

SG's Response
  • Mandatory packaging reporting
  • Disposable carrier bag charge (2023)
  • Extended Producer Responsibility for e-waste

⭐ Must-Know for Exams

  • Least serious air pollution effect on children: chest tightness
  • SG plastic recycling: 4% (2021)
  • Max home noise 1hr: 67 decibels
  • Dolphins/whales sensitive to noise: rely on sound for communication
  • Diurnal rhythm = 24-hour biological cycle
Week 8

Waste Management — Responsible Consumption & Production

Consumerism, material footprints, the production-consumption chain, and Singapore's Zero Waste vision.

♻️ SDG 12: Responsible Consumption 🗑️ Zero Waste 📦 Circular Economy
Global consumption is accelerating — driven by population growth, rising incomes, and retail culture. The current linear "take-make-dispose" model is unsustainable. Material footprints measure total resources extracted globally to satisfy demand. Singapore's Zero Waste Masterplan targets a circular approach — reduce waste, extend product lifetimes, and recover materials.
2 billion tonnes of solid waste generated/year
40% waste in SG is from food
2030 SG Zero Waste Masterplan target year
Semakau SG's only landfill — nearly full
🛒 Consumerism
Dominant Driver

Retailer satisfaction dominates consumerism — continuous promotion of buying more, newer, faster. Fast fashion is a prime example.

Why Unsustainable?

Products rely on too many natural resources and create harmful emissions when consumed or discarded. Industrialised countries' patterns are especially problematic.

Planned Obsolescence

Products designed to fail or become unfashionable quickly. Drives repurchase cycles and waste.

📊 Material Footprint
What it Measures

Total amount of raw materials extracted globally to meet a country's consumption demand. Different from ecological footprint.

Ecological Footprint (different!)

Measures human demand on nature — quantity of nature needed to support people. This is what material footprint is NOT.

🔗 Production & Consumption Chain
  • 1
    Extraction — mining, logging, fishing, farming for raw materials
  • 2
    Production — manufacturing, processing, refining
  • 3
    Distribution — transport, logistics, retail
  • 4
    Consumption — use by households and businesses
  • 5
    Disposal — landfill, incineration, recycling, composting
🇸🇬 Singapore's Zero Waste Masterplan
Core Aim

Adopt a circular economy approach to waste and resource management. Shift towards more sustainable production and consumption patterns.

3R Hierarchy
  • Reduce — most preferred (buy less)
  • Reuse — extend product life
  • Recycle — last resort before disposal
Semakau Landfill

SG's only and last landfill. Projected to be full by ~2035. Driving urgency for waste reduction and circular economy.

⭐ Must-Know for Exams

  • Dominant in consumerism: retailer satisfaction
  • Material footprint ≠ ecological footprint (ecological footprint measures nature demand — that's NOT material footprint)
  • Correct chain: Extraction → Production → Distribution → Consumption → Disposal
  • Unsustainable consumption: too many natural resources + harmful emissions
  • Zero Waste Masterplan aim: circular economy approach
Week 9

Sustainable Cities

Urbanisation trends, cities' environmental impact, the Urban Heat Island effect, and what makes a resilient, liveable city.

🏙️ SDG 11: Sustainable Cities 🌡️ Urban Heat Island 💪 City Resilience
Over 55% of humanity lives in cities — rising to 68% by 2050. Cities occupy only ~1% of land but consume 75% of energy and produce 70% of CO₂ emissions. This disproportionate impact makes urban sustainability central to global goals. Cities are simultaneously the problem AND the most powerful platform for sustainability solutions.
55%+ of humanity lives in cities now
1% of land but huge environmental footprint
75% energy consumed by cities
70% global CO₂ from cities
🌍 Urbanisation Trends
Disproportionate Impact

Cities occupy only ~1% of land globally yet consume the majority of resources and produce most emissions — a massive footprint mismatch.

Main Urban Impacts
  • High energy consumption
  • Transport emissions
  • Waste generation
  • Impervious surfaces → flooding
NOT a main urban impact

High use of agricultural pesticides/irrigation in urban farms — this is a rural/agricultural issue, not primarily urban.

🌡️ Urban Heat Island (UHI) Effect
How it Works

Dense urban areas with less trees + more concrete absorb and retain more heat than surrounding sea or forest. Cities are 2–5°C warmer than rural areas.

Causes
  • Dark surfaces absorb solar radiation
  • Lack of vegetation (no evapotranspiration)
  • Waste heat from buildings/vehicles
  • Dense building geometry traps radiation
SG Solutions
  • Greenery on building walls/rooftops
  • Street trees and parks
  • Cool paint & reflective materials
  • Urban ventilation corridors
💪 Resilient Cities
Definition

A resilient city: the city and its urban dwellers can continue to thrive even in a disaster. Ability to absorb, adapt, and recover from shocks.

Types of Shocks
  • Climate: floods, heat waves, sea level rise
  • Economic: financial crises
  • Social: pandemics, civil unrest
  • Physical: infrastructure failure
Why Cities Lead Sustainability

Cities don't need to reduce dependence on environment — they need to manage it sustainably. Cities ARE platforms for innovation & policy action.

🇸🇬 Singapore as a Sustainable City
City in a Garden

Singapore's vision — integrating greenery throughout the urban fabric. 350km of park connectors, green rooftops, nature ways.

Long-Term Plan 2030

Singapore Green Plan 2030: achieve net zero by 2050. Green buildings, solar panels, EV infrastructure, nature-based solutions.

⭐ Must-Know for Exams

  • Disproportionate city impact: 1% of land, huge emissions/energy
  • NOT a main urban impact: agricultural pesticides/irrigation
  • NOT true about cities: they don't need to urgently reduce environmental dependence (they need sustainable management)
  • UHI: less trees + more concrete → absorbs & retains more heat
  • Resilient city: city & dwellers continue to thrive even in disaster
Week 10

Biodiversity, Ecosystems & Sustainable Finance

The web of life, biodiversity threats, ecosystem services valuation, ESG investing, and green finance frameworks.

🦋 SDG 15: Life on Land 🐟 SDG 14: Life Below Water 💵 Green Finance
Biodiversity — the variety of life on Earth — underpins all ecosystem services that economies and societies depend on. The 6th mass extinction is underway, with species declining 60–70% since 1970. Sustainable finance — ESG criteria, green bonds, and impact investing — is re-routing capital toward sustainability. Singapore is positioning itself as Asia's green finance hub.
1M+ species threatened with extinction
60–70% wildlife decline since 1970 (WWF)
$44 trillion global GDP dependent on nature
ESG Environmental, Social, Governance
🌿 Ecosystem Services
Provisioning

Food, fresh water, timber, fibre, genetic resources, medicines. Direct material benefits from nature.

Regulating

Climate regulation, flood control, water purification, pollination, disease regulation.

Cultural

Recreation, spiritual, aesthetic, educational value. Tourism, mental health benefits.

Supporting

Soil formation, nutrient cycling, photosynthesis. Foundation for all other services.

🚨 Biodiversity Threats (HIPPO)
  • H — Habitat Destruction — deforestation, wetland draining, urban sprawl (#1 threat)
  • I — Invasive Species — out-compete native species, disrupt food webs
  • P — Pollution — chemical contamination, plastic, eutrophication
  • P — Population Growth — increased resource demand, land pressure
  • O — Overharvesting — overfishing, poaching, unsustainable logging
💵 Sustainable Finance
ESG Investing

Investment decisions based on Environmental, Social, and Governance criteria alongside financial returns.

Green Bonds

Fixed-income securities raising capital specifically for climate/environmental projects. SG issued its first sovereign green bond (2022, S$2.4B).

Impact Investing

Investments made with intention to generate positive, measurable social and environmental impact alongside financial return.

SG Green Finance

MAS (Monetary Authority of Singapore) driving green taxonomy, sustainability disclosure, and SG-Asia Green Finance Centre.

🌊 Ocean Sustainability
Ocean Acidification

Ocean absorbs 25% of CO₂ → forms carbonic acid → pH drops → dissolves coral skeletons, shellfish. Reef bleaching.

Overfishing

90% of fish stocks fully or over-exploited (FAO). Illegal, Unreported, Unregulated (IUU) fishing major problem.

⭐ Must-Know for Exams

  • Top biodiversity threat: habitat destruction
  • Ecosystem services: provisioning, regulating, cultural, supporting
  • ESG = Environmental, Social, Governance
  • Ocean absorbs CO₂ → ocean acidification → coral bleaching
  • 6th mass extinction ongoing: human-caused, primarily habitat loss

Circular economy approach. Semakau Landfill full ~2035. 3R: Reduce → Reuse → Recycle.

Black Soldier Fly

Larvae convert food waste into compost. Protein-rich byproduct for animal feed. Circular innovation.

Carbon Tax

$5/t (2019) → $25/t (2024) → $50–80/t (2030). Economy-wide, covers ~80% of SG's emissions.

Singapore Green Plan 2030

Net zero by 2050. Green buildings, 2GW solar by 2030, EV adoption, City in Nature.

📝 Key Practice Questions

All 45 official MCQs from the CC0006 Quiz — Weeks 1 to 9 — with toggle-reveal answers. Test yourself before checking!

W1
Why Everyone Should Know About Sustainability
1 The first time that countries worldwide signed a deal to work together to limit global warming, and made financial commitments to do so, was in 2015. What was the deal called? ▾
✅ Answer

The Paris Agreement.

2 What kind of human activity adds the most CO₂ to the atmosphere? ▾
✅ Answer

Combustion of fossil fuels.

3 Sustainable development spans many different areas and academic disciplines. Which option is NOT a characteristic of complex problems? ▾
✅ Answer

Solutions may be simple but hard to finance. (Complex problems do NOT have simple solutions.)

4 Singapore aims to transition towards a more circular economy. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of a circular economy? ▾
✅ Answer

Compensating for all emissions and pollution through, for example, tree planting or cleaning up plastic waste from benches. (That is offsetting/linear thinking, not circular.)

5 In what way is planting a million trees in Singapore a natural climate solution? ▾
✅ Answer

Trees help cool down the island naturally through shading and releasing water through transpiration.

No Poverty, Social Inclusion & Equality
1 No poverty, zero hunger, and reduced inequalities are some of the important sustainable development goals. True or False? ▾
✅ Answer

True.

2 Which of these regions are some of the most poverty-stricken regions of the world? ▾
✅ Answer

Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia.

3 A socially inclusive society aims to eliminate discrimination, enable high social mobility, and ensure that every human being can meet basic needs. True or False? ▾
✅ Answer

True.

4 Technology and the digital revolution are widening the income gap. True or False? ▾

True — the digital divide means those without access fall further behind economically.

5 Gender pay gap is the only aspect of gender inequality. True or False? ▾

W3
Food Security & the Global Food System
1 From 2019–2020, the number of undernourished people in the world: ▾
✅ Answer

Increased — COVID-19 pushed ~100 million more into food insecurity.

2 In 2020, the World Food Programme (WFP) was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize. What was the motivation? ▾
✅ Answer

"For its efforts to combat hunger, for its contribution to bettering conditions for conflict-affected areas..."

✅ Answer

A third of the food produced — approximately 1.3 billion tonnes per year.

4 In a successful project in Singapore, the larvae of Black Soldier Flies are used to: ▾

Convert food waste into nutritious compost. They also produce protein-rich larvae usable as animal feed.

5 How much of habitable land is used to grow crops for human consumption and for livestock respectively? ▾

27% of habitable land is used for livestock and 7% to grow crops for human consumption.

1 Which is the factor that makes water demand difficult to predict? ▾

Climate change — unpredictable rainfall patterns, droughts, and floods make future demand impossible to forecast accurately.

2 How many people worldwide face high levels of baseline water stress? ▾

One-quarter (approximately 2 billion people).

3 Which economic sector consumes the most water worldwide? ▾

Agriculture — accounts for approximately 70% of global freshwater withdrawals.

50%.

5 Which one is NOT a national tap of Singapore? ▾

Imported water from the Strait of Johor. (Singapore imports water from the Johor River, not the Strait of Johor — which is sea water.)

W5
Energy & Climate Change (I)
1 People from the high-income countries are: ▾
✅ Answer

Looking for further improvement in well-being (not basic access — they already have that).

2 CO₂ is the only greenhouse gas. True or False? ▾
✅ Answer

No — other major GHGs include methane (CH₄), nitrous oxide (N₂O), and fluorinated gases.

3 What is 'energy poverty'? ▾
✅ Answer

Lack of access to sustainable modern energy services and products.

4 Once emission mitigation measures are implemented, adaptation plans will need to be scrapped. True or False? ▾
✅ Answer

False — both mitigation and adaptation are needed simultaneously. Mitigation reduces future warming; adaptation deals with impacts already locked in.

5 Advanced technologies, behaviour change, clean energy, and fuel shift are important for emission mitigation. True or False? ▾
✅ Answer

True — all four are key pillars of the emission mitigation toolkit.

W6
Energy & Climate Change (II) — Policy & Economics
1 Which of the following are economic incentive-based policy instruments? ▾
✅ Answer

Market-based tools such as cap and trade, and other non-market mechanisms such as carbon tax.

2 Which of the following statements is true about Cap and Trade? ▾
✅ Answer

Cap and Trade ensures that the polluter pays and allows for mitigating emissions at the lowest cost possible.

3 Which statement is true about CO₂ emissions in 2018? ▾
✅ Answer

China emitted more than the United States. (China ~28%, USA ~15% of global CO₂ emissions.)

4 The Stern Review suggested: ▾
✅ Answer

Taking climate change action is a pro-growth strategy — the cost of inaction (5–20% of GDP) far exceeds the cost of action (~1% of GDP).

5 When was the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) signed? ▾
✅ Answer

1992 — at the Rio Earth Summit (United Nations Conference on Environment and Development).

W7
Pollution — Air, Light, Noise & Plastic
1 What is the least serious effect of air pollution on children's health? ▾
✅ Answer

Chest tightness. (More serious effects include reduced lung function, heart disease, and cancer.)

2 What percent of its plastic does Singapore recycle as of 2021? ▾
✅ Answer

4%.

3 The maximum permissible noise level to which a Singaporean working at home can be exposed for one hour is about _____ decibels. ▾
✅ Answer

67 decibels.

4 Why are dolphins and whales extra sensitive to noise pollution? ▾
✅ Answer

They rely on sound for communication (and navigation/echolocation). Noise pollution interferes with their ability to find food, navigate, and communicate.

5 Light pollution causes a disruption in our diurnal rhythm. Which of the following best describes the diurnal rhythm? ▾
✅ Answer

Our body's 24-hour biological cycle — regulating sleep, hormones, metabolism, and other physiological processes.

W8
Waste Management — Responsible Consumption & Production
1 Which of the following is an area which dominates consumerism? ▾
✅ Answer

Retailer satisfaction — constant promotion of buying more, faster, and newer drives consumerism.

2 Which of the following statements is NOT related to material footprints? ▾
✅ Answer

"It measures human demand on nature — the quantity of nature it takes to support people or an economy." (That is the ecological footprint, not the material footprint.)

3 Which of the sequences about the production and consumption chain is correct? ▾
✅ Answer

Extraction → Production → Distribution → Consumption → Disposal.

4 Why are consumption patterns in most industrialised and developed countries considered unsustainable? ▾
✅ Answer

Their products and services rely on too many natural resources and create harmful emissions when consumed or discarded.

5 What is the aim of the Singapore Zero Waste Masterplan? ▾
✅ Answer

Adopting a circular economy approach to waste and resource management practices, shifting towards more sustainable production and consumption.

W9
Sustainable Cities
1 In what way is the environmental impact of cities disproportionate? ▾
✅ Answer

Cities occupy only about 1% of land globally but have a large impact on the environment (consuming 75% of energy, producing 70% of CO₂).

2 Which of the following is NOT one of the main ways that cities impact the environment? ▾
✅ Answer

The high use of agricultural pesticides and water for irrigation in urban farms. (This is an agricultural/rural issue, not a primary urban environmental impact.)

3 Which one is NOT true? Cities play a critical role in advancing the sustainability agenda because: ▾
✅ Answer

"Cities urgently need to reduce their dependence on the environment." (This is NOT true — cities need to manage and use nature sustainably, not reduce dependence. They depend on and should protect ecosystems.)

4 How does the 'Urban Heat Island Effect' work? ▾
✅ Answer

A dense urban environment, with less trees and more concrete, absorbs and retains more heat than the surrounding sea or forest.

5 What is meant by a 'resilient city' in the context of urban sustainability? ▾
✅ Answer

The city and its urban dwellers should be able to continue to thrive even in a disaster.
