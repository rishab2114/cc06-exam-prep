# 12 — Launch Strategy for NTU

Marketplaces die from **cold-start / liquidity** problems, not from lack of features. The launch plan is engineered around solving the chicken-and-egg in the smallest possible arena.

## Strategy: Go hyper-narrow, then widen
Don't launch "all of NTU" on day one. Launch **2–3 adjacent halls** (a "hall cluster") and own them completely before expanding hall-by-hall.

### Why a hall cluster
- Tasks are inherently local (walk to a room, collect a parcel). Density within walking distance = fast fill = good first experience.
- A cluster (~2,000–3,000 students) is small enough to saturate with grassroots tactics, large enough to generate daily demand.

### Recommended beachhead
Pick a high-density cluster near amenities (e.g., the Crescent/Pioneer/Tamarind area or Halls 8–11) — high parcel volume (collection demand), shared laundry (laundry demand), inspection cycles (cleaning demand).

## Phase 0 — Pre-launch (Weeks -4 to 0)
- **Manual liquidity (concierge MVP):** before the app is polished, run a WhatsApp/Telegram broadcast group + Google Form to fulfil real tasks manually. Learn pricing, demand mix, fulfilment time. This both validates and seeds early supply.
- Recruit **20–30 reliable "founding Buddies"** (providers) from the cluster — pay guarantees for first shifts so demand always gets filled.
- Pre-verify their IDs so supply is instant at launch.
- Line up **launch demand events:** exam season, hall inspections, move-in/move-out week, festive parcel surges.

## Phase 1 — Seed the cluster (Weeks 1–4)
- **Supply first, lightly subsidized:** earnings guarantees / bonus for first N completed tasks; "refer a Buddy" bonus.
- **Demand kick:** first-task discount/credit (e.g., S$5 off first task) funded as CAC; flyers on hall notice boards, lift posters, table-drops at dining halls, hall WhatsApp groups, CCA chats.
- **Ambassadors:** 1–2 paid student ambassadors per hall (hall councils, RAs) — they have distribution we can't buy.
- **Always-fill guarantee:** if no Buddy accepts within X minutes, ops fulfils manually so the customer experience never breaks. This is the single most important early tactic.

## Phase 2 — Saturate & widen (Weeks 5–12)
- Expand to adjacent halls once fill rate > 70% and median time-to-accept < 15 min in the beachhead.
- Lean into **recurring demand**: inspection-week cleaning campaigns, exam-week food/laundry pushes, move-out week.
- Turn on **referral loops** for both sides; reward completed-task referrals only (anti-gaming).
- Start **category sequencing**: launch with parcel/laundry/food (low-risk, high-frequency), add cleaning (T2), defer airport/cooking (T3).

## Institutional relationship (do this early — see docs/16)
- Engage **NTU Student Affairs Office, hall councils, and the students' union** *before* aggressive growth. Offer to be a sanctioned, safety-first service rather than a rogue app they ban.
- Position around **student income + safety + verification**; share the safety layer and insurance plan.
- Explore official partnership: featured in hall onboarding, integration with hall events.

## Channels (ranked by expected CAC efficiency on campus)
1. Hall WhatsApp/Telegram groups + hall councils (near-zero CAC).
2. Student ambassadors / RAs.
3. Physical: lift posters, notice boards, dining-hall table tents, washing-room stickers (laundry context).
4. Campus IG meme pages / NTUSU channels (paid shoutouts).
5. Launch events tied to demand spikes (move-in, inspections, exams).

## Launch metrics & go/no-go to widen
- Fill rate > 70%, median time-to-accept < 15 min (peak).
- ≥ 1 completed task/active user/week in beachhead.
- CSAT (post-task) ≥ 4.5/5; dispute rate < 3%; **zero unaddressed safety incidents**.
- Provider weekly retention > 40%.

## Budget sketch (first 12 weeks, lean)
- Supply guarantees + bonuses: S$4–6k
- Demand credits (first-task): S$3–4k
- Ambassadors (4–6 students): S$3–5k
- Print/marketing: S$1–2k
- Insurance (PLI) + legal review: S$5–10k (see docs/13/16)
- Total: ~S$16–27k to prove a cluster.
