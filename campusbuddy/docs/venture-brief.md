# CampusBuddy — Venture Brief

*On-demand campus help — by students, for students.*
*A verified-student marketplace for chores and errands. Singapore, multi-campus.*

---

## The problem
Students living on campus are time-poor (lectures, labs, internships, CCAs, exams)
and often without transport. Everyday chores pile up — laundry, parcel pickups,
food runs, pre-inspection cleaning. The current options are bad: **asking a friend**
(not always available, you owe them a favour, they flake) or **free/paid Telegram
groups** (sketchy, unreliable, no accountability).

At the same time, plenty of students want **flexible income** between classes.

## The product
Post a task → a **verified student from your campus** accepts and does it → both
sides rate each other. Categories: **laundry, parcel/errand runs, grocery & food
runs, a spare home-cooked meal, room cleaning, room/move-out help, and peer study help (tutoring).**

What makes it different from a chat group:
- **Verified students only** — gated by campus email + matric verification.
- **Arrival check-in** — at the door, the buddy verifies via NTU pass or a live
  in-app matric photo, so the person who shows up is the one you accepted (no
  sending a substitute).
- **Safety by design** — same-gender option for personal tasks; cleaning is done
  with the customer present; **laundry is contactless** (bag left outside) with
  Grab-style live status updates.

## Spotlight service — move-out & shifting help
Helping students **move and carry belongings** at room change / move-out — carrying,
packing, and shifting boxes/luggage, often as a two-buddy job. This is a **labour
service only** — CampusBuddy does not hold/store anyone's items (staying true to the
help-marketplace idea, not becoming a storage operator).

Why it's a strong service:
- **High value per job** (S$15–40+) vs micro-errands — better economics and worth a buddy's time.
- **Seasonal surge** at end-of-semester move-out, hall reallocation, and break-time
  storage — predictable demand spikes to market around.
- **Pairs with storage**: connects to the break-time pain of "where do I keep my
  stuff?" — a natural B2B tie-in with local self-storage providers.
- **Lower risk**: the customer is present and moving their own things, so it avoids
  the alone-in-a-room concern.

## Why people pay instead of asking a friend
1. **On demand** — a buddy is available when you need one.
2. **No social debt** — pay a fair price, owe no one, use it as often as you like.
3. **Trust** — verified + rated + accountable, which informal channels can't match.

> We're not creating demand — people already pay for informal gigs today. We're
> moving that demand onto a **trusted, reliable rail**: more trustworthy than
> Telegram, more reliable than a friend.

## Business model
- **Commission: deliberately low** (progressive ~1–5%, capped) — an *adoption lever*,
  not the business. Kept low to stay "cheaper, by students."
- **Advertising is the real engine.** A verified 18–24 student audience is premium
  inventory: F&B near campus, banks (student accounts), telcos, tuition, gyms, events.
  Ads are clearly labelled and monetise **attention**, so the model is robust even if
  users settle payment off-app.
- **Later:** buddy subscription, featured listings, B2B partnerships (laundromats, halls).

The North Star is therefore **verified-student engagement (weekly active use)** —
not take-per-transaction.

## Market
All Singapore tertiary students (~**250k+** incl. polytechnics & private): NUS ~38k,
NTU ~33k, SUSS ~16k, SMU ~12k, SIT ~10k, SUTD ~1.5k, + ~95k polytechnic + large
private. **Vision: all campuses;** the playbook ports regionally (Malaysia, India).
**Launch: one campus at a time** — liquidity *and* local advertisers are per-campus.

## Honest assessment
This is a **strong, focused small venture** — not a unicorn. Single-population campus
markets cap transaction volume, and a low take + ad model is a real but modest
business: one engaged campus can do roughly **S$80k–150k/yr** from ads at ~10k active
students; a handful of campuses → a mid-six-figure business that funds a small team.

The advantages of treating it as a small venture: keep commission low without
investor pressure, control the regulatory pace, and win deep local trust before
scaling.

**Key risks (and the plan):**
- **Engagement** — a once-a-week utility must become a daily open (deals/events feed).
- **Regulation** — student work-pass limits (MOM), hostel commercial-activity rules,
  food-safety (SFA) for home meals, PDPA for verification data. *Mitigation:* launch
  with a university's blessing; start with low-risk, no-room-entry services.
- **Disintermediation** — mitigated by keeping engagement (and ads) in-app.

## What success looks like (and the next step)
Success = **weekly repeat usage**, not signups. The cheapest way to prove it, before
scaling the app:

> **3–4 week manual pilot, one campus, one service (laundry).** A Telegram/WhatsApp
> group + a form, matched by hand. Target: **~10 jobs/week and >40% of users
> returning.** If it hits, the venture is real and we scale the app; if not, we've
> learned it for ~S$0.

**Status:** working app prototype built (verified onboarding, task posting, bidding,
contactless laundry tracking, in-person cleaning check-in, ad surface). The product
is ahead of the proof — the pilot is the priority.
